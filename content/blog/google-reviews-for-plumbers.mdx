Plumbing sits at an interesting review intersection. You're one of the most-needed emergency services in residential contracting, which means high volume — but most plumbing emergencies are stressful events for homeowners, which makes the ask more delicate. At the same time, non-emergency plumbing (water heater installs, repiping, drain work) is exactly the kind of job where a satisfied homeowner is happy to share their experience.

The challenge is calibrating your ask strategy to the job type — and building a system that captures the non-emergency volume automatically while handling emergency timing carefully.

## Ask timing for plumbing job types

**Emergency repairs (burst pipes, slab leaks, flooding):** These are your most emotionally charged jobs. The homeowner is stressed during the call, potentially dealing with water damage, and focused on minimizing disaster — not evaluating their experience. The right ask timing:

- Day 1: no ask — they're still in crisis mode
- Day 2 (next morning): if the repair is confirmed solid and no further issues have emerged, send a follow-up check-in: "Wanted to make sure everything's still holding tight since yesterday. Hope the [repair] is solid. If we took good care of you, we'd really appreciate a quick Google review — here's the link."
- If there were complications (secondary damage, multiple visits): wait until everything is fully resolved

**Water heater replacements:** Ask at job completion, after you've verified the unit is running and the homeowner has hot water. The result is immediate and tangible. Conversion rates on water heater installs are higher than most plumbing job types because the customer satisfaction is clear and complete.

**Drain cleaning and service calls:** Ask immediately at job completion — the drain is clear, the result is visible, the homeowner is satisfied. Standard in-person ask at job close, automated follow-up the next day.

**Repiping projects:** These are large, multi-day projects that build strong relationships. The ask timing is after project completion and pressure testing, when the homeowner can confirm everything is working. These jobs convert at high rates — the investment and the relationship are both significant.

## Platform priorities for plumbers

**Google Business Profile — primary and non-negotiable**

Homeowners searching "plumber near me" or "emergency plumber [city]" see Google's local pack first. Your review count, rating, and recency all feed that ranking. Everything else is secondary.

**Google Local Services Ads — reviews visible in the ad unit**

If you run LSA (the Google-screened badge ads that appear above regular ads), your review count and rating are displayed directly in the ad. More reviews = higher ad rank = more impressions. This is a direct revenue connection between your review volume and your paid traffic.

**Yelp — minimal investment for most plumbing markets**

Yelp is particularly weak for plumbing — homeowners in emergency situations go to Google first, not Yelp. In most markets, Yelp reviews in plumbing drive minimal lead volume. Don't invest in active Yelp review collection; let organic reviews come if they come.

**Angi — worth attention if you buy their leads**

If you're buying Angi leads or running Angi Ads, your Angi rating directly affects lead quality. Ask Angi-sourced customers specifically for an Angi review in addition to Google.

## How to respond to price complaint reviews

Plumbing's "invisible work" problem (inside walls, under slabs, behind fixtures) makes price sensitivity particularly acute. A $2,500 main line replacement that homeowners can't see feels expensive even when it's fair market rate. Price complaints in reviews are predictable — the smart move is to have your response template ready.

**Response framework for price complaints:**
1. Acknowledge the investment ("You're right that [job type] is a significant expense")
2. Explain the value concisely ("We use [material/method] to ensure it holds long-term without a return visit")
3. Take responsibility for the communication ("If the pricing wasn't fully clear upfront, that's on us and I apologize for that")
4. Offer to discuss offline ("If you'd like to talk through the specifics, please reach out directly at [contact]")
5. Close professionally ("We stand behind our work and want to make sure you're satisfied")

Never dispute the price in public, never share customer details (even to defend yourself), and never imply the customer is wrong to be frustrated. Future homeowners reading the exchange are evaluating your professionalism — that's what they're buying.
