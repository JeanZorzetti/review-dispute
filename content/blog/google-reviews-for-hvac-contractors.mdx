HVAC contractors have a structural advantage over most other trades: you interact with customers multiple times per year, on routine maintenance visits, seasonal tune-ups, emergency calls, and system replacements. Every one of those interactions is a review opportunity. An HVAC company running 20–30 service calls per week has 80–120 review opportunities monthly — a pace that can produce a 200-review profile in less than a year if the system is working.

This guide covers the HVAC-specific playbook: job-type timing, how to handle emergency calls, platform priorities, and what a working monthly volume looks like for your market.

## HVAC review timing by job type

<AnswerBox>
HVAC's review timing varies more by job type than any other trade. Service calls warrant an immediate ask at job close. Large system installations work better 24–48 hours post-commissioning, after the customer has experienced the new system running. Emergency calls require waiting until the homeowner is out of crisis mode — asking while the AC is still out or while they're still stressed about the bill produces poor conversion and occasional hostile responses.
</AnswerBox>

**Service calls and tune-ups:** Ask immediately at job close, while the tech is still on-site. The homeowner can feel the result — the AC is blowing cold, the furnace is heating, the system passed inspection. This is peak satisfaction. "Glad we could get that sorted out. If you have a minute, a quick Google review would help us a lot — here's the link right now."

**System replacements and installs:** The best ask is a follow-up call or text 24–48 hours after commissioning. "Just checking in — how's the new [system] running for you? If everything's good and we took good care of you on the install, a Google review would mean a lot." This timing catches customers after they've experienced the system and confirms satisfaction before asking — higher quality reviews, higher conversion.

**Emergency calls (no-cooling in August, no-heat in January):** Wait until the emergency is fully resolved and the homeowner has had time to recover. An emergency call ask the same day — while the homeowner is still stressed and processing an unexpected expense — converts poorly. Follow up 48 hours later when they're comfortable: "Glad we could get there quickly on Tuesday — hope the [system] has been running well since. If we took good care of you, we'd really appreciate a Google review."

**Maintenance contracts and recurring visits:** Existing contract customers are excellent review candidates but require a different approach. Don't ask every visit — that's annoying. The right moment is 2–3 visits into a contract when the relationship is established, or after you resolve a service issue for them. Ask once, follow up once, then don't ask again for 12 months.

## Platform priorities for HVAC

**Google Business Profile — everything flows through here**

Every HVAC lead channel — Google Ads, local SEO, Google Local Services Ads — ties to your GBP. Review count, rating, recency, and response rate all feed your local ranking. This is your primary reputation asset.

**Angi — critical if you buy their leads**

Angi's lead quality algorithm surfaces contractors with higher ratings to homeowners evaluating bids. If you spend on Angi Leads, your Angi rating directly affects the quality of leads you receive. Ask Angi-sourced customers for both a Google and Angi review.

**Google Local Services Ads (LSA) — reviews feed your LSA ranking directly**

If you run Google LSA ads (the "Google Screened" badge ads), your review count and rating are displayed in the ad unit and are a direct input to your ad rank. This is one of the few places where more reviews literally means cheaper leads and more impressions.

**Yelp, Facebook — low priority for most HVAC markets**

Unless your market has unusually high Yelp usage (some West Coast metros), Yelp isn't worth active investment. Facebook reviews matter for brand social proof but don't drive lead generation the way Google does.

## The HVAC conversion benchmarks

| Job type | Expected conversion rate | Volume example |
|---|---|---|
| Service call / repair | 8–14% | 25 calls/week → 2–3 reviews/week |
| Seasonal tune-up | 6–10% | 40 tune-ups/week → 2–4 reviews/week |
| System installation | 15–25% | 5 installs/week → 1–1.5 reviews/week |
| Emergency call | 5–9% | 10 emergencies/week → 0.5–1 review/week |

An HVAC company running these volumes has the potential for 6–10 new reviews per week without any paid tools — just a consistent in-person ask and a 24-hour automated follow-up.

## Protecting your profile from competitor attacks

HVAC is a competitive trade, particularly in metro markets where consolidation has created large regional players competing against independent contractors. Competitor-sourced fake reviews are more common in HVAC than most contractors realize.

Warning signs of a coordinated attack:
- Sudden cluster of 1-star reviews within 24–48 hours
- Reviewers with no history or accounts created recently
- Generic complaints with no job-specific details
- Timing coincides with a competitor campaign or your peak season

If you see these patterns, document them, report each review through your GBP dashboard, and consider engaging a specialist. [How to remove a fake Google review](/blog/how-to-remove-fake-google-review) covers the full removal process.
