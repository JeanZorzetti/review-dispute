This is the story of a Dallas-area roofing contractor — 12-person crew, primarily residential replacements and storm repairs — who built a review generation system from scratch and grew their Google profile from 14 reviews at 3.8 stars to 89 reviews at 4.7 stars in six months.

The goal isn't to present a magic result — it's to show exactly what they changed, why it worked, and what any roofing contractor can replicate.

## Starting situation

In January 2026, the company had the following review profile:

| Metric | January 2026 |
|---|---|
| Total reviews | 14 |
| Average rating | 3.8 stars |
| Reviews in past 6 months | 2 |
| Monthly completed jobs | ~28 |
| Review conversion rate | ~1% |

Two of the 14 reviews were one-star complaints from 2024 — one from a customer dispute that had been resolved, one that reviewers suspected was a competitor attack. Those two reviews were pulling down the average and sitting near the top of the profile due to sorting by relevance.

The company wasn't asking for reviews systematically. They sent a generic "How did we do?" email a week after every invoice — but the email contained no review link, just a satisfaction rating widget, and the response rate was under 5%. Virtually none of those respondents ended up on Google.

## What changed: the 5-component system

**Component 1: Generated a direct Google review link**

The owner logged into Google Business Profile, clicked "Get more reviews," and copied the direct review link. He ran it through bit.ly to create a short URL and generated a QR code. Total setup time: 20 minutes.

**Component 2: Created leave-behind review cards**

The company printed 100 business-card-sized "leave-behind" cards with the QR code on one side and a brief message on the back: *"If we took care of you today, a 30-second Google review means more to us than any marketing budget. Thank you."* Cost: $28 at a local print shop.

**Component 3: Implemented the in-person walkthrough ask**

Every project manager was given a one-sentence script: *"Really glad this came out the way we planned. Quick favor — if you have 30 seconds tonight, a Google review helps us more than you'd think. Here's a card with the link, and I'll text it to you now so it's on your phone."*

The PM then texted the short URL directly from their personal phone while still standing with the homeowner.

**Component 4: Replaced the generic email with a text follow-up**

The office manager sent a personalized follow-up text the morning after each job close:
*"Hi [Name] — [PM Name] from [Company] here. Really glad the roof came out well. If you haven't had a chance yet, here's the Google review link: [link]. Totally optional, but it means a lot. Thanks!"*

If no review appeared in 5 days, one additional follow-up was sent. After that, no further contact.

**Component 5: Established a daily review check and response habit**

The owner set a 10-minute daily alarm to check for new reviews. Response was required within 24 hours of any new review — positive or negative.

## Month-by-month results

| Month | New reviews | Running total | Average rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| January (baseline) | 2 | 14 | 3.8 ⭐ |
| February | 8 | 22 | 3.9 ⭐ |
| March | 11 | 33 | 4.1 ⭐ |
| April | 13 | 46 | 4.4 ⭐ |
| May | 14 | 60 | 4.6 ⭐ |
| June | 15 | 75 | 4.7 ⭐ |
| July | 14 | 89 | 4.7 ⭐ |

The improvement in average rating came from two sources: new genuine 5-star reviews from satisfied customers, and a ReviewShield case initiated for one of the two legacy 1-star reviews. The competitor-attack review was identified as a fake (reviewer had no job record, name matched a social media account linked to a competing company) and removed in month 4. The second 1-star — a genuine complaint that had been resolved — remained on the profile but became less prominent as positive volume grew.

## Business impact

By month 6, the company was ranking in the local pack for "roofing contractor Dallas" and related queries in several zip codes where they hadn't appeared before. Lead call volume from Google increased.

The project manager who was initially resistant to the walkthrough ask reported that it took "about 10 seconds" per job and that homeowners almost never reacted negatively to the request — most said "of course" or "already planning to."

## What any roofing contractor can replicate today

**This week (2 hours total):**
- [ ] Generate your direct Google review link from GBP dashboard
- [ ] Shorten it and create a QR code
- [ ] Order review cards (Vistaprint, local printer)
- [ ] Write the walkthrough ask script and share with your PMs

**This month (ongoing):**
- [ ] Make the in-person ask at every walkthrough
- [ ] Send a text follow-up the morning after every job close
- [ ] Set a daily 10-minute review check and response reminder
- [ ] Track reviews vs. completed jobs in a simple spreadsheet

The system cost this company nothing for the first three months. The improvement was entirely from consistent execution of a process that takes under 2 minutes per job.
