This case study covers a Phoenix HVAC contractor — 8 technicians, primarily residential service calls, tune-ups, and system replacements — who activated review automation that had been sitting unused in their Housecall Pro account for over a year.

Starting point: 31 reviews, 4.1 stars. Nine months later: 178 reviews, 4.8 stars. Local pack ranking: from alternating #4–6 to consistent #1.

The story is less about discovering new tools and more about actually using what was already available.

## Starting situation (October 2025)

| Metric | October 2025 |
|---|---|
| Total reviews | 31 |
| Average rating | 4.1 stars |
| Reviews in past 6 months | 5 |
| Monthly completed jobs | ~85 (service calls + installs) |
| Review conversion rate | ~0.6% |
| Local pack position (primary keywords) | #4–6 |

The company had been on Housecall Pro Grow for over a year. During onboarding, someone had started the review request setup but never completed it — the feature was enabled but the SMS template was blank and the trigger was misconfigured to fire on "estimate sent" rather than "invoice paid."

The 31 existing reviews came from customers who left feedback unprompted — a 0.6% spontaneous review rate on 85 monthly jobs.

## What changed

**Step 1: Fixed the Housecall Pro configuration (Day 1)**

The owner spent 90 minutes in Housecall Pro's review settings:
- Changed trigger from "estimate sent" to "invoice paid"
- Wrote a new SMS template with the customer's name, the review link, and a brief personal message
- Set delay to 24 hours after payment
- Added a 5-day follow-up sequence

**Step 2: Added in-person ask for system installations (Week 1)**

For service calls, the automation handled the ask. For system installations (the larger, higher-satisfaction jobs), the owner added a personal ask at the commissioning walkthrough. Script:

*"Before I get out of your hair — if you're happy with how the install went, a Google review would mean a lot to our company. I'll text you the link right now while I'm here. Takes 30 seconds."*

Installation conversion rate after adding the in-person ask: 19% (versus 11% for service calls with automation only).

**Step 3: Set up daily review response habit (Week 1)**

Created a phone notification for new GBP reviews. Owner committed to responding to every review within 24 hours. AI drafts (ChatGPT) were used to generate response drafts, reviewed by the owner before posting.

## What drove the ranking improvement

The owner tracked his local pack position weekly by searching "HVAC contractor Phoenix" and the 3 primary neighborhood keywords from an incognito browser window. The improvement followed the review volume growth with approximately a 4–6 week lag — consistent with how Google's local algorithm incorporates new review signals.

Three factors the owner believes drove ranking:
1. **Review count** — from 31 to 178 in 9 months put them well above the #2 and #3 competitors in count
2. **Recency** — consistent 15–17 new reviews per month vs. competitor profiles that hadn't earned a review in 60+ days
3. **Response rate** — responding to 100% of reviews within 24 hours vs. competitors with no responses

## One removal case

In month 5, a cluster of 3 one-star reviews appeared within 48 hours. None of the reviewer names matched any customer record in Housecall Pro. Review content was generic ("terrible service," "unprofessional") with no job-specific details.

A ReviewShield case was opened. Two of the three reviews were identified as fake non-customer reviews based on account age, review history, and lack of any matching customer record. Both were removed within 3 weeks. The third review — which on closer inspection described a situation that matched a resolved service call — was left in place and responded to professionally.

The two removed reviews would have dropped the average from 4.8 to approximately 4.6 if left standing at that review count. The owner noted that having them removed "felt like the right outcome — those were never real customers."

The results in this case came primarily from activating a tool that was already paid for and unused. The lesson isn't that Housecall Pro is magic — it's that the ask system, not the tool, is what drives review volume. This company had 85 jobs per month and was converting 0.6% of them. With a working system, they converted 11–19% of those same jobs. The volume was always there; the system just wasn't capturing it.
