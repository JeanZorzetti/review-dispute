Electrical work carries a trust dimension that most other trades don't: homeowners are worried about safety and licensing in a way they're not when they hire a plumber or landscaper. This makes your review profile a more careful document than most — homeowners reading your reviews are specifically looking for signals about reliability, licensing, and whether they can trust you inside their home's electrical system.

This guide covers the electrician-specific review playbook: when to ask, how to handle safety complaints, what the platforms prioritize, and how to protect your profile from false safety claims.

## Ask timing for electrical job types

**Panel upgrades and new circuits (permit required):** The ideal ask moment is when the homeowner receives the passing inspection certificate or when you confirm the inspection passed. "Good news — inspection passed this morning. Everything's up to code and you're good to go. If we took good care of you through the process, a Google review would mean a lot — here's the link." This timing leverages the official validation as a confidence signal.

**Outlet replacements, fixture installs, troubleshooting (no permit):** Ask at job completion when you've verified the circuit is working and the homeowner is satisfied. Standard in-person ask with a text follow-up the next morning.

**Whole-home rewiring projects:** These are large, multi-day jobs that build strong relationships. Ask after project completion and final walkthrough, when all circuits are verified and the homeowner has lived with the results for a day. High conversion — the investment is significant and the relationship is deep.

**EV charger installations:** A growing category with high homeowner satisfaction. Ask at completion — EV charger installs have some of the highest review conversion rates because the homeowner is excited about the new capability and satisfied with a modern, clean installation.

## Responding to safety and licensing reviews

Safety reviews are your most critical response priority. Homeowners researching electricians will read your responses to safety complaints carefully before deciding to call. A poorly handled safety complaint does more damage than the original review.

**Response framework for safety complaints:**
1. State your license status factually ("We are licensed electrical contractors in [state], license #XXXXXX")
2. Address the specific concern without being defensive ("I'd like to understand exactly what aspect of the work concerned you")
3. Note your inspection record ("All our permitted work is third-party inspected by the local building department")
4. Offer to resolve offline ("Please contact us directly so we can address your specific concern")
5. Keep it professional and brief — don't over-explain

**For false safety claims:** If a review claims your work is unsafe or unlicensed and the reviewer was not a customer (or the claim is fabricated), this may violate Google's policy. Document: screenshot the review immediately, check your job records for any matching customer, and report the review through GBP for policy violations. False claims about licensing or safety that are posted by non-customers are among the strongest removal cases under Google's fake-review policy.

## Platform priorities for electricians

**Google Business Profile — primary lead source for residential**

"Electrician near me" and "licensed electrician [city]" are Google searches. Your GBP review profile is the primary decision-making document for residential homeowners.

**Google Local Services Ads — "Google Screened" badge matters here**

Google LSA for electricians includes a background check ("Google Screened") verification that appears in the ad. Homeowners respond strongly to this badge for trade categories involving safety. If you run LSA, your review count and rating directly affect your ad placement.

**Angi — relevant for jobs requiring contractor evaluation**

Angi's marketplace is used more heavily by homeowners doing careful contractor evaluation for larger projects. If you do significant project-based work (panel upgrades, rewires), an Angi presence with reviews adds credibility.

**Yelp, Facebook — low priority for most electrical markets**

Yelp has minimal presence in most electrical contracting markets. Facebook reviews matter for brand awareness but don't drive lead generation.
