In March 2026, a Chicago-area plumbing company with a strong review profile was targeted by what appeared to be a coordinated fake-review attack. Six one-star reviews appeared over 36 hours, dropping their average from 4.7 to 3.9 stars — a shift visible enough to affect their local pack position within days.

This case study covers the full arc: recognizing the attack, working with ReviewShield to pursue removal, the timeline, and what the company did to protect themselves going forward.

## Starting situation and the attack

**Before the attack (early March 2026):**

| Metric | Early March 2026 |
|---|---|
| Total reviews | 87 |
| Average rating | 4.7 stars |
| Local pack position | #2 for "plumber Chicago [neighborhood]" |
| Monthly new reviews | 4–6 (spontaneous, no ask system) |

The company had built their 87-review profile organically over 6 years without a systematic ask process — primarily through spontaneous reviews from satisfied customers. A solid profile, but fragile: no ongoing generation meant they were one bad-review cluster away from a visible rating drop.

**The attack (March 14–15, 2026):**

Over 36 hours, six one-star reviews appeared. Warning signs were immediate:
- All 6 appeared between Thursday evening and Saturday morning
- 5 of 6 reviewers had Google accounts created within the past 2 weeks
- None of the reviewer names matched any customer in the company's ServiceTitan records
- Review content was generic ("terrible," "no-show," "ripped me off") with no job-specific details
- One review mentioned "the receptionist named Jennifer" — the company has no receptionist named Jennifer

**Impact:**

| Metric | March 16, 2026 (post-attack) |
|---|---|
| Total reviews | 93 (87 real + 6 fake) |
| Average rating | 3.9 stars (down from 4.7) |
| Local pack position | Dropped to #5–7 within 72 hours |

## The ReviewShield removal process

**Week 1: Evidence gathering**

The owner contacted ReviewShield immediately. Evidence compiled:
- Screenshots of all 6 reviews with reviewer profiles
- ServiceTitan records showing no matching customers for any of the 6 reviewer names, dates, or addresses
- Account creation dates for each reviewer (all within 2 weeks of posting)
- Note about the "Jennifer" reference confirming at least one review was fabricated
- Screenshots of the company's other Google reviews for comparison (to establish the anomalous pattern)

**Week 2: Cases submitted**

ReviewShield submitted removal requests for all 6 reviews, citing:
- Fake reviews from non-customers (no customer record, fabricated details)
- Coordinated inauthentic behavior (cluster of reviews from new accounts within 36 hours)
- Specific content violations (factual inaccuracies, fabricated employee names)

**Weeks 3–4: First removals**

The 3 clearest cases — accounts with no review history, created within days of posting, review content with factual errors — were removed within 3 weeks of submission. Average rating improved from 3.9 to 4.3 stars.

**Weeks 5–6: Remaining cases**

Two remaining reviews required escalation beyond the initial report — Google's initial automated review found no violation. ReviewShield submitted additional documentation including the account creation dates and the pattern evidence (coordinated timing). Both reviews were removed in week 6. One review remained: a review from an account with longer history, where the customer claimed a no-show for an estimate that couldn't be definitively disproven (no ServiceTitan record for an estimate call that may have been informally scheduled). This review was responded to professionally and left in place.

**Final removal result:** 5 of 6 reviews removed. Average rating after removals: 4.6 stars (down 0.1 from pre-attack due to the remaining legitimate complaint).

## What they built concurrently: the generation system

While the removal process was underway, the company built a review generation system from scratch. Previously, their 4–6 monthly reviews were entirely organic. Post-attack, they implemented:

- ServiceTitan Marketing Pro automation (they were already on ServiceTitan, upgraded to Marketing Pro)
- In-person ask by technicians on all service calls and installs
- Response habit: owner responds to every review within 24 hours

**Generation results over 3 months post-attack:**

| Month | New reviews | Running total | Average rating | Local pack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| April 2026 | 13 | 101 | 4.4 ⭐ | #3–4 |
| May 2026 | 15 | 116 | 4.5 ⭐ | #2–3 |
| June 2026 | 16 | 132 | 4.6 ⭐ | #1–2 |

The combination of removals plus new positive volume accelerated rating recovery significantly. The remaining genuine 1-star complaint, which would have been visible at 87 reviews, became much less impactful at 132 reviews with a consistent generation rate.

## What the company changed permanently

**1. Review generation system:** The attack exposed the fragility of relying on organic reviews alone. A profile built over 6 years with 87 reviews is resilient to normal complaint patterns but vulnerable to coordinated attacks. An ongoing generation system (15+ reviews/month) builds the volume that makes any single attack less impactful.

**2. Weekly review monitoring:** The attack went unnoticed for 12 hours before a customer called to mention it. The company now has GBP notifications enabled and checks for new reviews every morning.

**3. Competitor monitoring:** They suspect the attack came from a direct competitor based on timing (immediately after a bid loss) and the geographic pattern of the fake accounts. They now track their top 3 local competitors' review activity monthly.

**4. ReviewShield on retainer understanding:** The company knows that if a future attack occurs, they have an established relationship and process for removal. The evidence-gathering process (ServiceTitan records, account screenshots) is now documented as a playbook.
